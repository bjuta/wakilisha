# The Sixty-Year Test

## A question from the future

If someone reads this decision sixty years from now, will we still be proud of it?

That is the Sixty-Year Test.

It is not for every small choice. Not every button label needs to carry the weight of history. But the important decisions do.

What WAKILISHA preserves.

How it names people.

How it explains culture.

How it uses AI.

How it treats community memory.

How it handles uncertainty.

How it expands across relationships.

How it records corrections.

How it chooses between noise and depth.

Those decisions should be able to face time.

## Why sixty years

Sixty years is long enough for fashion to pass.

It is long enough for the founders to be gone from daily decisions.

It is long enough for today’s music to become archive.

It is long enough for a small page to become a source someone depends on.

It is long enough for a careless claim to become inherited error.

It is long enough for a careful note to become historical evidence.

The Sixty-Year Test forces WAKILISHA to respect time before time has passed.

## What future readers deserve

Future readers deserve more than files.

They deserve context.

They deserve source discipline.

They deserve to know what was certain and what was not.

They deserve to see relationships that were visible at the time.

They deserve correction when the institution learned better.

They deserve language that did not reduce people to content.

They deserve an archive that treated culture as lived work, not decorative material.

WAKILISHA should build for them too.

## The test

Ask this before major decisions.

Did we preserve context?

Did we tell the truth?

Did we respect the people represented?

Did we avoid easy distortion?

Did we show evidence?

Did we name uncertainty?

Did we make relationships visible?

Did we leave better questions behind?

Would this still make sense after the trend fades?

Would someone from the culture recognize the care?

Would a future researcher understand what we meant?

Would we be embarrassed if this became the source people cited?

## Product implications

The Sixty-Year Test should shape product priorities.

A page that cannot preserve context should not pretend to be an archive.

A chart that cannot explain method should not act like a final measure.

An AI summary that cannot show its evidence should not become public authority.

A comment system that cannot preserve useful contribution should not claim to build community memory.

A Library that cannot distinguish public, internal, embargoed, and draft material cannot be trusted with institutional memory.

A product built only for speed will not pass the test.

## Editorial implications

Editors should imagine being cited later.

Would the sentence still feel fair?

Would the claim still feel careful?

Would the absence of evidence be visible?

Would the person described feel reduced or respected?

Would a future reader understand why this mattered in its moment?

This does not mean editorial work should become stiff. It means it should be durable.

The best writing can be alive now and useful later.

## Community implications

Community memory can become historical material.

A comment written today may be the only record of how people first experienced a song in a specific place.

That means WAKILISHA should treat useful contributions with care. It should make them findable, attributable where appropriate, and separable from noise.

It should also protect people. Not every memory should be exposed without consent. Not every contribution should become institutional material automatically.

The Sixty-Year Test asks for care, not extraction.

## AI implications

AI should be judged by time too.

Will this generated summary mislead future readers?

Will this inferred relationship become false memory?

Will this filled-in biography erase uncertainty?

Will this moderation decision misunderstand language and leave harm behind?

Will this automated question trail guide inquiry, or scatter attention?

AI is fast. Time is slow.

WAKILISHA should answer to the slow one.

## The courage to leave gaps

Some of the most important future decisions will involve leaving space.

Space for missing evidence.

Space for future correction.

Space for community memory.

Space for a question that has not been answered yet.

Space for complexity that cannot be flattened today.

A gap can be a sign of care if it is named honestly.

False completion is the greater danger.

## Failure modes

WAKILISHA is drifting when it chooses short-term attention over future trust.

It is drifting when it publishes claims it would not want cited later.

It is drifting when it treats culture as disposable.

It is drifting when AI fills gaps that should remain open.

It is drifting when corrections are treated as inconvenience.

It is drifting when the product forgets that today’s pages may become tomorrow’s archive.

## Closing note

The Sixty-Year Test is not about being grand.

It is about being answerable.

WAKILISHA should make decisions that can stand in front of time and say: we were not perfect, but we were careful. We tried to tell the truth. We tried to preserve context. We tried to leave better questions for the people who came after us.
