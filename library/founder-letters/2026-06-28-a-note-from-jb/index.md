# A Note from JB

If you're reading this, there's a good chance we've never met.

That thought makes me smile.

When I started WAKILISHA in 2019, I wasn't trying to build an institution.

I was trying to solve what I thought was a simple problem.

I wanted Kenyan artists to have a place to sell merchandise.

That idea didn't survive very long.

Reality taught me something more important.

The problem wasn't merchandise.

The problem was that we hadn't yet learned to value our own culture enough.

That realization changed everything.

WAKILISHA slowly became something else.

First it became a blog.

Then it became a place to document music.

Then artists.

Then songs.

Then relationships.

And eventually I realized that none of those things were the destination.

They were simply different ways of answering questions that mattered.

You'll notice something as you read these constitutions.

Many of them begin with questions instead of answers.

That isn't an accident.

Questions built WAKILISHA.

Whenever reality proved us wrong, we changed our minds.

Whenever evidence contradicted our assumptions, we followed the evidence.

I hope we never stop doing that.

If you're here many years after these words were written, you'll almost certainly disagree with some of our decisions.

Good.

You should.

Culture changes.

Knowledge grows.

The only thing I ask is that you remain faithful to the reason these documents exist.

Leave WAKILISHA more truthful than you found it.

Leave the cultural record clearer than you inherited it.

Ask better questions than we knew to ask.

If you do that, then you'll be building the same WAKILISHA I hoped for, even if it looks completely different.

— JB

Founder
