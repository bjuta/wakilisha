# WAKILISHA Magazine — Issue 009
## Art Direction Brief

**Art Director:** Muiruri Beautah  
**Creative School:** Folk Vernacular × Editorial Magazine  
**Mode:** Light  
**Season:** Ninth issue — the ground issue  

---

## Creative Direction

Place is not decoration. It is the source.

Issue 009 is grounded. It looks and feels like Nairobi — not a Nairobi that has been cleaned up for export, but the city as those who know it experience it: warm, dense, patterned, handmade, alive. The design borrows from sign painting, from kanga textiles, from matatu graphics, from the way information is printed on market packaging.

This is not appropriation. This is recognition. Wakilisha is from here. This issue looks like it.

---

## Typography

- **Display:** Fraunces — 700 italic. Rich, expressive, slightly irregular in its optical weight
- **Body:** Fraunces or Libre Baskerville — warm serif, generous line height
- **Mono:** DM Mono — for metadata only
- **Irregularity:** Acceptable. This issue does not need Swiss precision. Asymmetric type arrangements are expected.

---

## Colour

Warm earthy base (#FBF4E8). Amber accent (#D4832A). Secondary warmth: ochre, forest green, terracotta. These are the colours of Nairobi's walls.

The palette should feel like it was mixed from pigments found nearby, not selected from a Pantone library.

---

## Pattern Language

Pattern here is structural content — textile logic embedded in the layout. Options:
- A kanga border device at the edge of section openers
- Repeating text-as-pattern: the issue title printed small across a background area
- Corner frames derived from West African textile geometry
- Stamped initials as section markers

These are used where they have meaning, not as decoration. The pattern must come from the content, not be applied over it.

---

## Photography Direction

Documentary photography with warmth. Real spaces, real people, actual light. No flash photography. No studio lighting. Natural, golden, slightly overexposed — the way photos look when you take them in the afternoon in Nairobi.

The archive wall spread in this issue uses existing photographs as a mosaic — a wall of images that together build a sense of place.

---

## Spreads to Include

1. **Cover** — Warm earthy background, Fraunces italic title, seal as stamp
2. **Archive Wall** — A grid of images that together form a visual record of place
3. **Editor's Note** — Opens with a memory or observation about a specific place
4. **Pattern Field** — A spread where pattern itself is the rest note
5. **Feature** — Dense, warm, richly typeset. Drop caps in warm amber.
6. **Section openers** — Each one invokes a place. "Nairobi. The music district. Saturday afternoon."
7. **Article lists** — Dense cards, warm tones, image-forward
8. **Back matter** — A coordinates label, a place name, a stamp

---

## Voice Notes

Writing for this issue should carry the texture of being somewhere specific. Not "the music scene" — "the scene at Pawa254, on a Thursday night when the PA cut out." Not "the community" — "the 47 musicians who showed up when the venue closed."

Place, time, specific observation. The folk vernacular tradition: knowledge that comes from being there.

---

## The Vernacular Test

Does this spread feel like it could only come from here?  
Or does it feel like it could be from anywhere?  

If it could be from anywhere — if it has no geographic specificity, no cultural situatedness — it's not folk vernacular. It's global minimalism with a warm palette. That's different.
