## Creative Direction

The information structure is the design. Clarity is a moral commitment, not a style.

Issue 008 is the issue where data becomes editorial material. Charts, rankings, indexes, timelines — these are not supporting elements here. They ARE the feature. Numbers are first-class typography.

Edward Tufte, Jacques Bertin, the New York Times graphics desk, the early Isotype charts. That tradition, applied to African cultural data.

---

## Typography

- **Display:** IBM Plex Sans — 600 Semi-Bold for display, 300 Light for supporting labels
- **Body:** IBM Plex Sans — Regular 400
- **Mono:** IBM Plex Mono — mandatory for all data labels, numbers, legends
- **Principle:** Every number in this issue is set in IBM Plex Mono. Every label is set in IBM Plex Sans.
- **No italic except for axis labels and annotations**

---

## Colour

Light data field (#F4F6FA). Accent: #1E7BC4 (information blue). Secondary accents: red (#E84040) for negative trends, green (#2A8A3C) for positive trends.

The grid lines are visible in this issue: faint horizontal rules at every column, creating a ledger-like structure. This is intentional. The grid is the information structure.

---

## Data Visualisation Direction

Every piece of chart/graph content in this issue must:
1. Have a clear, labelled legend
2. Use consistent colour encoding (blue = neutral, red = decline, green = growth)
3. Have annotations that explain the insight, not just label the data
4. Be beautiful *because* it is clear, not in spite of it

The data visualization spread should be the centrepiece of this issue — the place where the reader says "I see it now."

---

## The Grid as Visible Structure

Unlike other issues where the grid is invisible scaffolding, in Issue 008 the grid is slightly visible — background lines create a ledger or graph-paper texture at very low opacity (2–4%). This is the information design tradition: the structure is part of the message.

## Spreads to Include

1. **Cover** — Numbers as the primary visual element. The issue's most important statistic at poster scale.
2. **Data Visualization** — A centrepiece chart or data narrative
3. **Number Monument** — A huge number as the editorial centrepiece
4. **Editor's Note** — Opens with a data observation, not a feeling
5. **Signal** — The cultural intelligence spread at maximum information density
6. **Section openers** — Grid-structured, number-led
7. **Article lists** — Clean information hierarchy: title, author, reading time, section
8. **Back matter** — Issue stats presented as a small data table

---

## Voice Notes

Writing for this issue should follow the same principles as the design: clear, evidential, specific. Numbers belong in the text. "Several artists" should be "17 artists." "Many venues" should be "the 23 venues we tracked."

If the text can't support a number, question whether the claim is actually evidenced.

---

## What This Issue Is Not

Not a dashboard. Not a report. Not an analytics product.

The data here serves narrative. The charts tell stories. The numbers have cultural meaning. This is data journalism in the tradition of the best New York Times graphics work — where clarity and beauty are not in opposition.
