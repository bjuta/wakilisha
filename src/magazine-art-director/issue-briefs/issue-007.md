# WAKILISHA Magazine — Issue 007
## Art Direction Brief

**Art Director:** Muiruri Beautah  
**Creative School:** Modernist Poster × Bauhaus  
**Mode:** Dark  
**Season:** Seventh issue — the statement issue  

---

## Creative Direction

Each spread is a single public argument. Not a page in a system.

Issue 007 is the most theatrical issue in the cycle. Every spread has one large decision. The headline is the image is the argument. If you find yourself adding a second point to a spread, you have two spreads.

This is the issue where the magazine operates like a series of posters hung in a gallery. You should be able to read the cover from 10 metres. The feature opener should work without the body copy. The section openers should each function as standalone works.

---

## Typography

- **Display:** Space Grotesk — 700 Bold for declarations, 300 Light for contrast
- **Body:** Space Grotesk — Regular 400
- **Mono:** Space Mono — for all metadata, section labels
- **Scale:** Theatrical. Headlines at 160–200px where possible. Small text should feel small.
- **Stacked display type:** Typographic blocks as poster stacks

---

## Colour

Deep dark (#08060C). Red accent (#FF3C34). No softness. High contrast blocks.

The red appears at maximum saturation. Not the warm amber-red of Issue 001, not the Swiss red of Issue 002. This is a poster red. Signal red. Stop-sign red.

---

## Composition Rules

Every spread: one dominant element. One supporting element. Nothing decorative.

The edge of the spread is active. Content can run to the edge, break the edge, use the edge as a tension.

---

## The Typographic Poster Spread

This issue features a mandatory full-viewport typographic poster spread: the cover title broken across two lines, each line filling the full width of the viewport. The letter as a visual argument. No imagery. Just type and intention.

---

## Photography Direction

When photography appears: editorial crop, treated as graphic material. High contrast. Possibly duotone — the image should feel printed, not photographed.

The crop must be a decision. Not a face centred in a frame — a jaw at the edge of the frame, a hand that occupies the bottom third, a silhouette that breaks the horizon.

---

## Spreads to Include

1. **Cover** — Full-viewport title at poster scale. Geometric red element for structure.
2. **Typographic Poster** — The cover title repeated as a full-page type object
3. **Editor's Note** — Declarative opener: "This issue is an argument."
4. **Feature** — One strong image + one large headline. Body text is secondary.
5. **Full-Bleed Image** — Photography treated as a poster element
6. **Signal** — Numbers as structural typography
7. **Section openers** — Each one a poster: title + number, nothing else
8. **Back matter** — Single statement. Coordinates. Seal.

---

## Voice Notes

This is the issue for bold claims. The language should match the visual register: declarative, confident, sometimes provocative.

"The room has already changed" is a modernist poster headline. "Here's what happened" is not.

Every section opener should open with a claim that could stand alone. The body copy argues for it.
